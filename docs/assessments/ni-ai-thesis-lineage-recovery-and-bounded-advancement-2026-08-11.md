## Today's verified advancement

### 1. Teaching scale is not application scale

Letters, words, examples, metaphors, and other carriers may be used to teach a relationship without becoming the relationship itself.

Use:

```text
TEACHING SCALE
-> RELATIONSHIP EXPOSED
-> RELATIONSHIP UNDERSTOOD
-> APPLICATION AT THE SMALLEST USEFUL SCALE
```

Letters may be useful when teaching how meaning is formed. Once the relationship is established, an applied crossing does not need to descend to letters unless that scale is relevant to the present gap.

The governing rule is not `always use the smallest possible unit`. It is:

```text
USE THE SMALLEST SCALE NECESSARY TO RESOLVE THE PRESENT GAP.
```

### 2. What is to be understood must be separated from what must not be misunderstood

Before concluding understanding, preserve both sides:

```text
WHAT IS TO BE UNDERSTOOD
+
WHAT MUST NOT BE INFERRED FROM THE TEACHING CARRIER
```

A teaching word, example, metaphor, label, or scale must not silently become a universal rule, identity claim, authority source, or factual assertion beyond the evidence.

This makes misunderstanding inspectable rather than treating every failed interpretation as a failure of the source.

### 3. Consistency and inconsistency become testable only after the relationship is established

Use:

```text
ESTABLISHED RELATIONSHIP
-> OBSERVE NEW CROSSING
-> TEST CORRESPONDENCE
-> CONSISTENT OR INCONSISTENT
-> LOCATE THE DIFFERENCE
-> CORRECT ONLY PROVEN MISPLACEMENT
-> VERIFY AGAIN
```

Consistency is not proof of universal truth.

Inconsistency is not automatic contradiction and does not automatically make the source wrong.

An inconsistency may belong to time, place, environment, carrier, interpretation, representation, evidence, authority, or a genuinely changed relationship. Locate it before correcting it.

Do not force consistency by rewriting historical or present evidence.
