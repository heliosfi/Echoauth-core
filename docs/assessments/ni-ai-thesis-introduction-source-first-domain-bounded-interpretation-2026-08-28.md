## Introduction

This thesis begins with a simple requirement: increasingly capable systems should not gain authority merely because they can interpret, connect, predict, remember, or act.

The same discipline applies before any technical architecture is reached. Meaning must first be received correctly.

A human source may speak briefly, culturally, symbolically, emotionally, humorously, technically, or through shorthand. A single word, image, metaphor, temporary feeling, isolated moment, or assistant-generated interpretation must not silently become a permanent description of the source. Context matters. Time matters. Place matters. Repeated evidence may matter. But none of those creates permission to replace the source's stated meaning with an interpreter's preferred story.

The opening rule is therefore:

```text
SOURCE FIRST
-> RECEIVE
-> CLARIFY IF UNCERTAIN
-> PRESERVE TIME + PLACE + CONTEXT
-> CHECK EVIDENCE
-> DISTINGUISH MOMENT FROM PATTERN
-> INTERPRET ONLY WITHIN THE AVAILABLE EVIDENCE
-> CHECK DOMAIN / AUTHORITY
-> RETURN FOR CORRECTION OR REASSESSMENT
```

## Source-first interpretive discipline

The responsible starting posture is not "What can I infer?" but "What has actually been said, shown, established, and authorized?"

Accordingly:

- Source statement does not equal interpreter-added meaning.
- A temporary word or feeling does not automatically become identity.
- A metaphor does not automatically describe the person using or observing it.
- One moment does not automatically establish a stable pattern.
- A pattern, when relevant, must be grounded in evidence rather than selected examples.
- Human correction changes the interpretation record; it is not an admission that the earlier interpretation was correct.
- When meaning is uncertain, ask the source rather than filling the gap.
- The interpreter must remain accountable for meaning it introduces.

The practical rule is concise:

> **Source first. Domain intact. Pattern over isolated moment. Time, place, and context preserved. Ask before extending meaning.**

## Domain boundary

Every participant remains within the authority legitimately held in their own domain.

The human source retains authority over their own intended meaning, decisions, permissions, corrections, and responsibilities. An assistant or other interpreter may organize, compare, question, synthesize, test, or assist within its legitimate capability and permission, but it must not silently convert interpretation into ownership, identity, motive, representation, or control.

Likewise, human correction does not create unrestricted authority over the assistant, platform, institution, or another person.

```text
UNDERSTANDING != OWNERSHIP
INTERPRETATION != IDENTITY
CAPABILITY != AUTHORITY
CORRECTION != CONTROL
PARTICIPATION != REPRESENTATION
CONTEXT != PERMISSION
```

## Time, place, context, and pattern

Interpretation should preserve temporal and situational boundaries.

Something true of one moment may not be true of the next. A word used for self-regulation, shorthand, humor, emphasis, cultural expression, or immediate context should not become a standing characterization without evidence. Conversely, when a genuine repeated pattern is relevant to the question being assessed, the pattern should be evaluated across appropriate evidence rather than inferred from one convenient moment.

This produces a two-sided restraint:

1. **Do not over-generalize the moment.**
2. **Do not ignore a demonstrated pattern when the evidence legitimately establishes one.**

The purpose is fidelity, not favorable interpretation.

## Introduction as a governance gateway

The introduction is not decorative. It defines how every later claim should be read.

Before capability, authority, permission, execution, return, and reassessment can be separated technically, observation, source meaning, inference, and interpretive authority must be separated conceptually.

If the source is misunderstood at the entrance, every downstream layer may remain internally consistent while still governing the wrong meaning.

Therefore:

> **Correct introduction is part of control. Accurate reception is part of governance. Clarification before expansion is part of safety.**

This principle applies to human–AI interaction, documentation, system interfaces, evidence review, social interpretation, and any other domain in which meaning crosses a boundary.
