# NI AI Responsibility Map

## Status

Founder-authorized repository assessment.

Responsibility-mapping scope only.

This document does not authorize runtime, implementation, deployment, execution, trading, funding movement, autonomous action, command execution, or capability expansion.

## Governing baseline

NI AI is one intrinsically governed intelligence system.

The current responsibility names are:

- **Adumetric** — upstream asymmetric understanding, narrowing, specificity, and bounded preparation.
- **Hawk** — workflow control, routing, dispatch, state tracking, return, and transition release.
- **Saloherm** — downstream hermetic completion, local integrity, completion reporting, and stop.

These are not separate systems. They are distinct engineering responsibilities inside one governed operating identity.

## Mapping method

Each repository artifact is assessed by the responsibility it actually performs, not by its folder name or historical label.

The mapping sequence is:

1. identify what the artifact receives;
2. identify what it is allowed to determine;
3. identify what it must not determine;
4. identify what state it returns;
5. identify which responsibility owns that behavior;
6. preserve historical terminology where it records prior engineering understanding.

## Responsibility map

### Adumetric — upstream asymmetric responsibility

Current repository expressions include:

- meaning and context interpretation in `architecture/system-overview.md`;
- NI-AI interpretation language in `governance/principles.md`;
- requirement-definition reviews that identify what must exist before a later transition may be considered;
- evidence-package definitions, provenance requirements, ambiguity checks, and scope-definition records;
- founder-authorized review lanes that narrow a broad objective into an exact bounded task.

The strongest current canonical example is:

- `docs/control-gates/sniperbot-live-money-readiness-ladder-stage-3-entry-requirements-definition-review.md`

That document performs upstream work by establishing the exact subject, authoritative hierarchy, current state, prerequisites, exclusions, evidence requirements, authority requirements, stop conditions, and permitted future movement before any bounded task may be issued.

Assessment:

The repository already contains substantial Adumetric responsibility, even though it does not yet use the name. Its defining behavior is the conversion of broad intent into sufficiently specific, evidence-grounded, bounded requirements.

Boundary:

Adumetric does not authorize itself, execute the work, or treat interpretation as permission.

### Hawk — workflow-control responsibility

Current repository expressions include:

- sequencing review order, gate order, handoff order, checkpoint order, and next-lane order;
- preserving hold, halt, refusal, downshift, and non-action states;
- carrying authority and task-order state without expanding it;
- enforcing single-use, currentness, synchronization, checkpoint, and return-state discipline;
- routing work to the next legitimate lane only after the required state exists.

The strongest current canonical example is:

- `docs/control-gates/echoauth-ceg-movement-sequencing-boundary-review.md`

That document defines CEG as sequencing and order control only. It may preserve movement order, but it may not create permission, bypass authority, trigger execution, or expand scope.

Assessment:

CEG is the repository's clearest existing implementation-facing expression of Hawk-like workflow responsibility. The two names should not yet be collapsed automatically. CEG is an existing architectural term with a defined boundary; Hawk is the refined responsibility identity that controls workflow across the NI AI operating rhythm.

Required preservation:

Future refinement must determine whether Hawk governs CEG, is expressed through CEG, or names a broader workflow-control responsibility of which CEG is one existing mechanism. That relationship must be proven before canonical renaming.

Boundary:

Hawk controls workflow state and movement. It does not create upstream meaning, perform downstream completion, or become governance.

### Saloherm — downstream hermetic responsibility

Current repository expressions include:

- bounded execution review;
- file-bounded, command-bounded, reversible, reviewable task orders;
- exact allowed and prohibited actions;
- local integrity and non-expansion requirements;
- completion records and explicit stopping after the bounded responsibility is satisfied;
- separation of execution capability from authorization and from broader architectural interpretation.

The strongest current canonical example is:

- `docs/control-gates/echoauth-bounded-execution-non-authorization-boundary-review.md`

That document defines a constrained downstream zone after permission enforcement while refusing to infer active execution capability. It preserves the distinction between a bounded assignment and authorization to operate.

Assessment:

The repository already expresses the Saloherm discipline through bounded, non-expanding, exact-scope work. Its hermetic character appears in the repeated rule that a task may perform only its explicit responsibility and may not widen authority, capability, runtime, or scope.

Boundary:

Saloherm does not reinterpret upstream meaning, authorize itself, reconnect the whole architecture, or continue after completion.

## Cross-cutting governed responsibilities

### Requirements

Requirements are strongly represented throughout `docs/control-gates/`.

They are treated as conditions that must exist before a transition may be considered, not as suggestions and not as movement authority.

Repository evidence shows the pattern:

- define the exact subject;
- identify controlling sources;
- establish prerequisites;
- identify exclusions;
- require currentness, applicability, provenance, and internal consistency;
- preserve refusal and stop conditions.

This is aligned with the NI AI requirement that downstream receive sufficiently specific bounded work.

### Evidence

Evidence is treated as distinct from acceptance, authority, and execution.

The repository repeatedly preserves:

- provenance;
- exact file and checkpoint identity;
- currentness;
- acceptance status;
- conflict and ambiguity checks;
- independent verification;
- durable completion and disposition records.

Evidence reveals whether a relationship or gate condition is supported. It does not itself authorize movement.

### Authority

Authority remains explicit, current, scoped, traceable, and non-inferential.

Founder intent, readiness, evidence, chronology, repository cleanliness, indexing, or prior approval may not substitute for current explicit authority.

This responsibility remains separate from Adumetric interpretation, Hawk workflow control, and Saloherm completion.

### Continuity

Continuity is currently preserved through:

- canonical source hierarchies;
- checkpoint and branch binding;
- provenance and lineage requirements;
- preservation of prior FAIL and BLOCKED dispositions;
- prohibition on rewriting history;
- exact return and completion records;
- index synchronization.

Continuity is not created by governance. Governance protects continuity by preventing responsibility and authority drift.

### Legitimate movement

The repository already embodies the law that readiness is not authority, authority is not execution, and completion of one lane does not authorize the next.

Movement becomes legitimate only after:

- the specific requirements are established;
- evidence is sufficient and accepted;
- the proper authority is explicit;
- an exact bounded order exists;
- the responsible lane completes without scope expansion;
- the result is durably recorded and returned.

## Fast-forward and reverse mapping

### Fast forward

```text
Broad objective or reality
-> upstream interpretation and narrowing
-> exact requirements and evidence package
-> explicit authority
-> workflow sequencing and bounded dispatch
-> hermetic completion
```

### Reverse

```text
Hermetic result and completion report
-> workflow return and state preservation
-> evidence and continuity assessment
-> upstream reconnection
-> next legitimate transition, if supported
```

The repository contains both directions, but historically expresses them through governance lanes, task orders, result records, disposition records, and synchronization records rather than the current Fast Forward / Reverse vocabulary.

## Primary finding

The three refined responsibilities are not missing from the repository.

They already exist as distributed engineering behavior:

- Adumetric is visible in requirement discovery, source hierarchy, evidence definition, and bounded task preparation.
- Hawk is visible in sequencing, routing, checkpoint, state, handoff, return, and no-bypass controls.
- Saloherm is visible in exact bounded completion, non-expansion, local integrity, completion reporting, and stop.

The repository therefore needs responsibility alignment and canonical relational language, not three newly invented subsystems.

## Unresolved relationship requiring later assessment

The exact relationship among Hawk, CEG, workflow, and EchoAuth permission enforcement is not yet finalized by this mapping.

Current evidence supports:

- CEG performs movement-sequencing behavior;
- EchoAuth performs deterministic permission enforcement;
- Hawk controls workflow in the refined NI AI rhythm;
- workflow must carry state in both forward and reverse directions.

A later workflow assessment must determine the precise non-drifting relationship without collapsing workflow control into permission enforcement or treating all sequencing as Hawk's complete identity.

## Responsibility-mapping conclusion

The repository already has the engineering responsibilities required by the current NI AI operating posture.

The missing work is to connect those responsibilities system-wide so present-state canonical documentation consistently expresses one intrinsically governed intelligence system.

No historical artifact should be renamed or rewritten solely to match current terminology.
