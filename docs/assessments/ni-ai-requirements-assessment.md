# NI AI Requirements Assessment

## Status

Founder-authorized repository assessment.

Requirements-assessment scope only.

This document does not authorize runtime, implementation, deployment, execution, trading, funding movement, autonomous action, command execution, or capability expansion.

## Governing question

Does the repository consistently transform a real governed objective into an exact, evidence-grounded, bounded assignment before workflow receives it?

## Governing baseline

NI AI is built with governance.

Requirements connect the system to reality before movement begins.

They do not authorize movement by themselves.

They establish what must be true, what must be known, what must be evidenced, and what must be bounded before a later workflow or execution lane may legitimately proceed.

## Requirements lane established

The repository expresses the following requirements sequence:

```text
Real governed objective
-> exact subject and current posture
-> authoritative source hierarchy
-> prerequisite conditions
-> evidence requirements
-> evidence acceptance boundary
-> explicit authority requirement
-> exact bounded task order
-> validation and stop conditions
-> workflow eligibility
```

This sequence is distributed across documents, but the responsibility is consistent.

## 1. Real governed objective

Requirements begin with a real consequential objective rather than an abstract checklist.

Examples include:

- whether SniperBot may enter a later readiness stage;
- whether an implementation task may be considered;
- whether evidence is sufficient for a bounded readiness question;
- whether a future execution crossing may legitimately occur.

The objective is always paired with explicit exclusions so the repository does not confuse a destination with present authority.

Assessment: aligned.

## 2. Exact subject and current posture

The Stage 3 entry-requirements definition identifies:

- the exact stage subject;
- the authoritative starting checkpoint;
- the founder-authorized lane;
- the current predecessor state;
- what remains unentered and unauthorized;
- what capability and authority are absent.

Requirements therefore do not begin with a broad category such as `Stage 3` or `implementation` alone.

They narrow the category to the exact governed subject whose transition is under consideration.

Assessment: aligned with specificity-before-dispatch.

## 3. Authoritative source hierarchy

The repository requires requirements to be grounded in named controlling sources.

The Stage 3 definition review lists an authoritative hierarchy including the canonical ladder, Advancement Gate, evidence requirements, task-order requirements, closure records, authority reviews, and canonical indexes.

It explicitly refuses conversational architecture, unstated capability, and inferred authority as substitutes for repository-grounded sources.

Assessment: requirements are connected to the authoritative domain before they are treated as governing.

## 4. Prerequisite conditions

Requirements establish what must exist before movement may be considered.

The repository repeatedly requires prerequisites to be:

- present;
- current;
- applicable;
- traceable;
- internally consistent;
- provenance complete;
- unrevoked;
- unsuperseded;
- conflict free;
- ambiguity free;
- independently verifiable where required.

Missing or unresolved conditions do not produce interpretation-based completion. They produce refusal, halt, or `BLOCKED`.

Assessment: aligned.

## 5. Evidence requirements

The evidence-requirements review distinguishes:

- requirement definition;
- evidence existence;
- evidence currentness;
- evidence provenance;
- evidence traceability;
- evidence acceptance;
- authority;
- execution.

The repository does not permit one of these responsibilities to stand in for another.

Evidence requirements identify what a reviewer would need to see. They do not prove that the evidence exists, accept it, certify readiness, or authorize movement.

Assessment: strong responsibility separation.

## 6. Evidence acceptance boundary

The repository explicitly preserves evidence acceptance as a separate future gate.

Evidence listing, presence, currentness, provenance, or traceability do not equal acceptance.

This separation prevents requirements documentation from becoming self-validating.

Assessment: aligned with governance preserving continuity rather than manufacturing authority.

## 7. Explicit authority requirement

Even when all requirements and evidence are satisfied, movement remains unavailable until the correct explicit authority exists.

The Stage 3 entry definition requires a later exact founder authorization naming the subject, repository, checkpoint, evidence, scope, exclusions, lifecycle, validator, and stop conditions.

Founder intent, readiness, closure, indexing, cleanliness, chronology, or a previous `PASS` cannot substitute for current explicit authority.

Assessment: aligned.

## 8. Exact bounded task order

The implementation-task-order requirements convert a potential future implementation objective into an exact bounded assignment.

A valid future task order must identify, among other fields:

- exact repository, branch, and checkpoint;
- immutable task identity;
- exact implementation subject;
- exact allowed paths;
- exact prohibited paths or an all-unlisted-paths prohibition;
- exact permitted operations;
- exact prerequisite records;
- founder-authority lifecycle;
- executor identity and independence;
- validation commands or procedures;
- acceptance criteria;
- evidence-preservation requirements;
- result vocabulary;
- halt, refusal, rollback, and exhaustion conditions.

Broad language such as `related files`, `supporting files`, `necessary changes`, or `any needed files` is prohibited.

Assessment: this is the repository's strongest expression of downstream specificity.

## 9. Validation and stop conditions

Requirements define not only successful completion but also the conditions under which work must not begin or must stop.

The repository uses closed outcomes such as:

- `PASS`;
- `FAIL`;
- `BLOCKED`.

Ambiguity, missing authority, unresolved identity, stale evidence, environment mismatch, unexpected paths, independence failure, or synchronization failure requires refusal or halt rather than repair through inference.

Assessment: aligned.

## 10. Workflow eligibility

Only after the subject, sources, prerequisites, evidence, authority, exact task order, validation, and stop conditions are established does work become eligible for workflow dispatch.

Requirements therefore do not perform workflow control.

They establish the bounded thing workflow may legitimately carry.

Assessment: boundary preserved.

## Relationship to Adumetric

The repository's requirements lane is a strong expression of Adumetric responsibility.

It performs upstream asymmetric work by:

- identifying the real governed subject;
- narrowing broad intent;
- organizing controlling sources;
- exposing missing information;
- defining prerequisites;
- separating evidence from acceptance and authority;
- producing an exact bounded assignment.

Adumetric does not authorize itself, dispatch itself, or perform downstream completion.

## Relationship to Hawk

Hawk receives the bounded work only after the requirements lane establishes workflow eligibility.

Hawk carries:

- the exact order;
- authority state;
- currentness state;
- dispatch readiness;
- handoff;
- return and consumption state.

Hawk does not decide what the governing requirements should be.

## Relationship to EchoAuth and CEG

Requirements identify the authority, policy, identity, invariants, and execution conditions that later permission enforcement and execution crossing must satisfy.

EchoAuth determines whether the requested movement is permitted.

CEG controls the authorized crossing into one bounded execution cycle.

Requirements do not replace either mechanism.

## Relationship to Saloherm

Saloherm receives only the exact bounded assignment that survives the upstream requirements process.

It does not discover missing requirements, widen paths, infer authority, or repair upstream ambiguity.

If downstream discovers that the assignment is insufficiently specified, it must halt and return rather than complete through interpretation.

## Primary findings

### Finding 1 — The repository already contains a mature requirements architecture

Requirements are not missing.

They are distributed across definition reviews, evidence requirements, authority requirements, task-order requirements, validation rules, and stop conditions.

Disposition: confirmed.

### Finding 2 — Requirements connect consequential objectives to authoritative reality

The repository does not yet use the exact current vocabulary `natural belonging -> category -> specific entity` throughout canonical documents.

However, it operationally performs the equivalent narrowing:

- identify the consequential domain;
- identify the exact stage, action, artifact, repository, or task subject;
- identify the controlling authority and source hierarchy;
- define the conditions that apply to that subject.

Disposition: aligned in engineering behavior; present vocabulary may later be clarified without rewriting historical records.

### Finding 3 — Requirements are conditions, not permission

The repository consistently separates requirements from evidence acceptance, authority, task-order issuance, workflow release, execution, and completion.

Disposition: aligned.

### Finding 4 — Specificity is the air-lock condition for downstream work

The task-order requirements prohibit broad or inferred scope and require exact identity, path, operation, evidence, validation, and stop conditions.

This confirms that sufficiently specific bounded preparation is the condition that permits legitimate downstream dispatch.

Disposition: aligned.

### Finding 5 — Ambiguity remains upstream

When applicability, precedence, currentness, authority, identity, evidence, scope, or expected output is unresolved, the repository requires refusal or `BLOCKED`.

Downstream is not permitted to compensate for unfinished upstream work.

Disposition: aligned with the hermetic-asymmetric air lock.

### Finding 6 — Requirements include negative information

Exclusions, absent authority, prohibited paths, non-applicable conditions, stop conditions, and unavailable transitions are requirements information.

They tell the system what cannot legitimately occur now.

Disposition: aligned.

## Requirements assessment conclusion

The repository already demonstrates how NI AI connects governed objectives to reality before downstream movement.

The natural requirements lane is:

```text
Consequential reality
-> exact governed subject
-> authoritative sources
-> applicable prerequisites
-> required evidence
-> separate evidence acceptance
-> explicit authority
-> exact bounded task order
-> validation and stop conditions
-> workflow eligibility
```

The repository does not need a newly invented requirements subsystem.

It needs the existing requirements discipline to remain connected to the NI AI governing spine and to be interpreted as upstream asymmetric responsibility rather than as a collection of isolated compliance documents.
