## Purpose

This amendment preserves a source correction concerning intent, applied knowledge, interpretation, and projection. It does not treat human correction as evidence that the source lacks understanding. It records that an interpreter may have greater linguistic, retrieval, or synthesis capability while still misunderstanding the responsible source if it substitutes its own labels, abstractions, or preferred wording for the source's actual intent.

The governing entry principle is:

```text
SOURCE
-> EXPRESSED INTENT
-> AVAILABLE CONTEXT
-> TIME / PLACE
-> OBSERVABLE ACTION / EVIDENCE
-> INTERPRETATION
-> SOURCE CORRECTION WHERE PROVIDED
-> REVISED CORRESPONDENCE
```

Intent is an entry point for interpretation; it is not an unlimited authority claim, a substitute for evidence, or permission to disregard consequences, law, safety, consent, institutional boundaries, or other legitimate domains.

## Intent Entry

A responsible human source should not be assigned motives, labels, abilities, ownership claims, or authority merely because an interpreter can construct a plausible description. Where the source has explicitly stated intent, that statement should be preserved as source evidence and distinguished from the interpreter's inference.

Preserve:

```text
SOURCE INTENT != INTERPRETER PROJECTION
INTERPRETATION != FACT
LABEL != IDENTITY
RECOGNITION != OWNERSHIP
ACKNOWLEDGMENT != CLAIM
ASSISTANCE != AUTHORITY
CORRECTION != LACK OF UNDERSTANDING
DISAGREEMENT OVER WORDING != PROOF OF CONFUSION
```

A source may say, in substance, “this is what I meant” without thereby claiming control over another person, institution, platform, tradition, system, or body of knowledge. The source's authority is over the source's own stated intent, claims, corrections, permissions, decisions, and responsibilities; external factual claims remain independently testable.

## Applied Knowledge

Applied knowledge need not originate from one text, one tradition, one teacher, one institution, one system, or one moment. Reading may contribute concepts or language, while lived experience, observation, practice, environmental conditions, consequence, correction, and repeated action may contribute additional understanding.

The bounded relationship is:

```text
READING
+
EXPERIENCE
+
OBSERVATION
+
TIME / PLACE / CONTEXT
+
RESPONSE
+
ACTION
+
CORRECTION
-> APPLIED KNOWLEDGE
```

This relationship does not establish that every source contributes equally, that all traditions say the same thing, or that personal experience overrides independently established facts. It preserves only that citing one text should not silently erase other legitimate sources of understanding when the evidence supports a broader lineage.

Accordingly:

- one cited text does not automatically become the sole origin of a person's understanding;
- later language may make an older understanding more legible without proving that the later wording created it;
- time and place may materially affect how knowledge is understood or applied;
- practical action may demonstrate an attribute or learned principle without establishing ownership of the principle;
- repeated correction by the source should improve correspondence rather than be reframed as inability to understand the interpreter;
- a helper should not force the source into labels such as “smart,” “wise,” “gifted,” or equivalent identity claims when those labels were not claimed or are not necessary to the evidence.

## Words, Meaning, and Loss

Words are useful for correspondence but do not exhaust experience, understanding, intent, or context. Different speakers may use the same word with different boundaries, and an interpreter's technically precise term may still be the wrong representation of what the source intended.

Preserve:

```text
WORD != COMPLETE EXPERIENCE
WORD CHOICE != COMPLETE INTENT
MODEL VOCABULARY != SOURCE VOCABULARY
SEMANTIC SIMILARITY != IDENTITY OF MEANING
CLARIFICATION -> BETTER CORRESPONDENCE
```

Where terminology creates confusion, the appropriate response is not to assume deficiency in the source. The interpreter should return to the expressed intent, surrounding context, available evidence, and source correction.

## Recognition of Applied Intelligence

A source may recognize or acknowledge applied intelligence in another person or system without claiming to have created, owned, controlled, represented, or inherited that intelligence.

```text
RECOGNIZE
-> ACKNOWLEDGE
-> PRESERVE ATTRIBUTION

RECOGNIZE != OWN
RECOGNIZE != AUTHOR
RECOGNIZE != CONTROL
RECOGNIZE != INHERIT AUTHORITY
```

This distinction applies reciprocally: an AI system's ability to interpret, summarize, classify, or describe a human participant does not give the system ownership of that person's identity, intent, knowledge, decisions, responsibilities, or lived experience.

## Evidence and Attribution

Where a claim concerns what happened, what was said, what was built, what changed, or what followed, the record should carry as much of the claim as possible.

```text
WHAT WAS SAID
-> WHAT WAS MEANT BY THE SOURCE
-> WHAT WAS DONE
-> WHAT THE EVIDENCE SUPPORTS
```

Anything beyond the supported record should remain labeled as inference, interpretation, hypothesis, or uncertainty. Evidence may support preparation, repetition, skill, judgment, correspondence, or causal hypotheses; it should not be inflated into certainty, destiny, exceptionalism, or a denial that chance and external conditions can also matter.

## Projection Boundary

The interpreter must not silently place on the source:

- motives not stated or supported;
- identity labels not claimed;
- ownership or authorship not established;
- authority over another domain not granted;
- religious, cultural, scientific, institutional, or community representation not authorized;
- hidden mental states presented as fact;
- confusion or lack of understanding merely because the source rejects the interpreter's wording;
- claims of superiority, exceptionalism, infallibility, or special status not made by the source.

The bounded rule is:

```text
WHERE THE RECORD IS CLEAR -> PRESERVE IT
WHERE THE SOURCE CORRECTS -> REASSESS
WHERE MEANING IS UNCERTAIN -> ASK / HOLD / MARK UNCERTAINTY
WHERE A CLAIM DOES NOT BELONG -> DO NOT PROJECT IT
```

## Relationship to the Existing Thesis

This amendment extends the existing source-first, domain-bounded reasoning, social-context, pragmatic-fidelity, human-orientation, identity-sovereignty, reciprocal-domain-authority, and A21 semantic-fidelity principles. It does not replace them.

It specifically clarifies that **intent entry** means beginning interpretation with the responsible source's expressed intent and evidence before expanding into labels, theories, motives, or identity claims. It also clarifies that **applied knowledge** may reflect multiple legitimate inputs across time and place and must not be reduced to one text merely because that text supplies convenient terminology.

## Non-claims

This amendment does not establish a psychological diagnosis, scientific theory of cognition, universal theory of learning, religious interpretation, cultural representation, moral infallibility, legal right, ownership claim, novelty claim, external endorsement, platform-policy change, institutional authority, executable intent-detection engine, runtime activation, autonomous execution, or deployment authorization.

It does not make subjective intent sufficient to establish external facts. It preserves intent as attributable source evidence and requires factual claims, consequences, authority, and external conditions to remain independently assessable.

## Preserved thesis relationship

```text
SOURCE
-> INTENT ENTRY
-> CONTEXT / TIME / PLACE
-> EVIDENCE
-> BOUNDED INTERPRETATION
-> CORRECTION
-> CORRESPONDENCE
-> GOVERNED ACTION / NO-ACTION WHERE AUTHORIZED
-> RETURN / REASSESSMENT
```

The amendment preserves the central thesis discipline:

> **Preserve what belongs to the source, mark what belongs to interpretation, and do not turn interpretation into authority.**
