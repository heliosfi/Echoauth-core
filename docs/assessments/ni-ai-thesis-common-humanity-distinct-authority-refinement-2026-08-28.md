## Status

This record extends the evidence-bounded NI-AI future-capability thesis with a human-scale identity-and-authority refinement.

It does not establish a new runtime capability, political authority, legal interpretation, geopolitical prediction, or automatic implementation requirement.

## Public evidence basis

The refinement is grounded against established public international-governance principles rather than private interpretation alone.

The Charter of the United Nations simultaneously preserves international peace and security, friendly relations among nations, equal rights and self-determination of peoples, international cooperation, a coordinating role for common ends, and the sovereign equality of Member States.

The Universal Declaration of Human Rights separately states the common dignity and equal rights of human beings without making that common humanity equivalent to one governmental or jurisdictional authority.

Official references:

- United Nations Charter: https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter/full-text
- Universal Declaration of Human Rights: https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights/

## What was learned

A shared human identity does not require the erasure of distinct peoples, states, governments, offices, jurisdictions, or legitimate responsibilities.

```text
COMMON IDENTITY != COMMON GOVERNING AUTHORITY
SHARED RESPONSIBILITY != SHARED AUTHORITY
COOPERATION != SOVEREIGNTY TRANSFER
REPRESENTATION != OWNERSHIP OF THE REPRESENTED PARTY
INFORMATION PASSAGE != PERMISSION TO ACT
```

The useful question is not only `Who are we together?` It is also `Who is legitimately authorized to do what, for whom, within which boundary, and on what evidence?`

Those questions may correspond without becoming identical.

## Common Humanity / Distinct Authority Principle

> **Humanity may recognize a common identity and coordinate responsibilities across peoples, nations, states, governments, institutions, and officials without erasing their distinct identities, jurisdictions, or legitimate authorities. Unity establishes correspondence; it does not by itself transfer sovereignty or permission.**

This is a thesis principle and interface-governance proposition. It is not a claim that every political system recognizes authority in the same way.

## Human-scale responsibility map

```text
HUMAN BEING
-> FAMILY / COMMUNITY
-> PEOPLE / NATION
-> STATE
-> GOVERNMENT
-> OFFICIAL / DELEGATED ROLE
-> INTERSTATE OR INTERNATIONAL INTERFACE
-> HUMANITY / COMMON HUMAN INTEREST
```

The arrows indicate possible relationships and passage of information or responsibility. They do not imply that authority automatically flows in either direction.

## Tension interpretation boundary

This refinement does not reduce conflict between states to one cause. International tension may involve history, security, territory, resources, law, ideology, domestic politics, unequal power, competing interests, or failures of trust and communication.

The narrower governance insight is that tension can be worsened when identity, jurisdiction, responsibility, representation, authority, permission, evidence, and action are confused.

A coordination interface should therefore make those distinctions more visible rather than pretending that a common problem creates one common authority.

## Digital correspondence

```text
IDENTITY
-> ROLE
-> JURISDICTION
-> RESPONSIBILITY
-> EVIDENCE
-> AUTHORITY CHECK
-> PERMISSION
-> ACTION OR NO-ACTION
-> RETURN / REASSESSMENT
```

The interface may help preserve provenance, role attribution, responsibility boundaries, authorized passage, and reassessment. The interface does not become sovereign merely because it coordinates the parties.

## EchoAuth correspondence

```text
INFORMATION MAY MOVE
RESPONSIBILITY MAY BE COORDINATED
AUTHORITY MUST REMAIN ATTRIBUTABLE
```

An image, message, record, government communication, institutional statement, or digital representation may carry information across a boundary without itself creating authority in the recipient.

Likewise, multiple participants may share responsibility for a common problem without becoming one authority.

## Thesis insertion

> **Connection without absorption; coordination without implied authority transfer.**

For increasingly capable digital systems, the practical implication is that a useful interface should support correspondence across heterogeneous human and institutional domains while preserving the authority native to each legitimate boundary.

This extends the existing thesis proposition that bounded responsibilities may correspond without collapsing into one another.
