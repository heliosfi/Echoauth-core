## Governing baseline

NI AI is built with governance.

The governing spine now preserved by the repository is:

- reality establishes the subject;
- requirements identify what must become true;
- evidence reveals whether the governing relationships are supported;
- authority remains explicit and separate;
- workflow carries only eligible bounded work;
- EchoAuth produces the applicable authorization state;
- CEG protects the authorized execution crossing;
- Saloherm completes bounded work and stops;
- return records preserve the result;
- Adumetric reconnects only what the result legitimately establishes;
- governance protects every responsibility boundary and consequential transition.

The continuity question is:

> Does the repository preserve one governing identity across product language, canonical engineering documents, historical records, requirements, workflow, authorization, execution crossing, and completion without hidden architectural forks or responsibility drift?

## Continuity trace

### 1. Product identity continuity

The README now establishes NI AI as one intrinsically governed intelligence system and identifies Adumetric, Hawk, and Saloherm as distinct engineering responsibilities rather than independent systems.

This creates a present-state interpretive baseline.

Assessment: governing identity established.

### 2. Historical layer-language continuity

`governance/principles.md` and `architecture/system-overview.md` preserve earlier layer-oriented language:

- NI-AI as meaning and context interpretation;
- MCG as authority and judgment resolution;
- EchoAuth as authorization and enforcement;
- CEG as execution sequencing;
- execution components as bounded performers.

This language does not invalidate the current spine. It records an earlier, narrower engineering view of responsibilities that remain materially present.

Assessment: historical and present language are reconcilable, but not yet fully cross-referenced.

### 3. Responsibility continuity

The repository consistently preserves separation among:

- meaning;
- requirements;
- evidence;
- authority;
- authorization;
- workflow;
- execution crossing;
- bounded completion;
- audit and return.

The current names Adumetric, Hawk, and Saloherm increase responsibility precision without requiring historical terms to be erased.

Assessment: no evidence of duplicated governing systems.

### 4. Requirements continuity

The Stage 3 requirements definition and Requirements Assessment preserve a continuous upstream lane from real subject through category, specificity, source hierarchy, evidence threshold, authority requirement, and bounded task-order eligibility.

Requirements stop before permission or execution.

Assessment: continuity preserved.

### 5. Workflow continuity

The workflow assessments establish a full forward-and-return rhythm while preserving that workflow does not create meaning, permission, or completion.

Hawk names the broader workflow responsibility. CEG retains its distinct execution-crossing identity.

Assessment: continuity preserved after correction of the earlier premature CEG placement.

### 6. Authorization continuity

EchoAuth remains the deterministic authorization and enforcement responsibility.

It does not become all governance, all workflow, or all intelligence.

The concise statement `governance permits` remains compatible with the more precise engineering relationship in which EchoAuth emits the bounded authorization state after evaluating authority, policy, identity, invariants, and runtime conditions.

Assessment: product and engineering language are compatible when their descriptive level is made explicit.

### 7. Execution-crossing continuity

CEG remains Crossroad Execution Gate.

Its historical and current evidence consistently preserve:

- valid authorization as a prerequisite;
- single-use execution control;
- replay prevention;
- payload, channel, and concurrency integrity;
- proceed, refuse, hold, halt, and revoke outcomes.

CEG neither becomes Hawk nor loses its relationship to the broader workflow.

Assessment: identity and boundary preserved.

### 8. Hermetic completion continuity

The repository repeatedly expresses bounded execution through exact allowed actions, prohibited actions, closed result vocabularies, non-expansion, durable reporting, and stop conditions.

Saloherm is a current responsibility name for this downstream discipline, not evidence of a newly invented subsystem.

Assessment: continuity preserved.

### 9. Reverse-path continuity

Disposition, completion, synchronization, and index records preserve more than audit history.

They reconnect bounded results to the larger system while preserving:

- lineage;
- consumed authority;
- non-replay;
- exact consequence;
- unavailable next movement.

Assessment: the reverse path is continuous with the governing spine.

### 10. Governance continuity

The Governance Assessment confirms that governance is present throughout the system as boundary and transition protection without replacing any responsibility.

The control matrix strengthens this by defining control as structural non-reachability of unsafe transitions.

Assessment: governance identity is continuous from product statement to implementation criterion.

## Findings

### Finding 1 — One governing spine is visible across the repository

The repository does not reveal multiple competing architectures.

It reveals multiple historical and engineering descriptions of one governing concern: preserving legitimate movement from interpreted reality into bounded consequence.

Disposition: confirmed.

### Finding 2 — The principal continuity gap is canonical cross-reference, not architecture

Current top-level identity language and older layer-oriented canonical documents are compatible, but their relationship is not always explicit.

A reader may still mistake NI AI for only a meaning layer or mistake EchoAuth authorization for the whole of governance.

Disposition: later localized canonical clarification is justified; broad rewriting is not.

### Finding 3 — Historical records must remain historical records

The source journals, prior boundary reviews, FAIL dispositions, BLOCKED dispositions, and earlier architectural terms preserve the development lineage that made the current understanding possible.

Updating current canonical interpretation must not rewrite or cosmetically modernize historical evidence.

Disposition: preserve unchanged.

### Finding 4 — Current responsibility names should be introduced relationally

Adumetric, Hawk, and Saloherm should be connected to demonstrated repository behavior rather than used to mass-rename files, layers, or historical mechanisms.

Disposition: preserve current names as responsibility identities; avoid unsupported term replacement.

### Finding 5 — No hidden fork was found between requirements, governance, workflow, authorization, CEG, and completion

The evidence supports distinct but connected lanes.

Apparent tension came primarily from descriptive level and timing, not incompatible architecture.

Disposition: continuity confirmed.

### Finding 6 — The repository's strongest continuity mechanism is non-substitution

The same rule recurs across domains:

- interpretation is not permission;
- evidence is not acceptance;
- readiness is not authority;
- authority is not execution;
- workflow is not governance;
- authorization is not completion;
- completion is not next-lane authority;
- documentation is not runtime effect.

Disposition: treat non-substitution as a canonical continuity law.

### Finding 7 — The governing spine can absorb correction without reconstruction

The CEG reassessment corrected one unsupported relationship while preserving product identity, workflow, authorization, and downstream boundaries.

This is evidence that the architecture is stable under new evidence.

Disposition: confirmed.

## Continuity risks

The remaining risks are interpretive rather than structural:

1. current readers may treat older layer language as the complete product identity;
2. concise product language may be mistaken for complete engineering mechanics;
3. new responsibility names may be treated as new components;
4. historical documents may be rewritten instead of cross-referenced;
5. successful completion may be misread as automatic next movement;
6. canonical present-state documents may drift if they are updated independently without spine validation.

## Continuity preservation rules

- Preserve the README as the current product-identity baseline.
- Preserve historical artifacts without retroactive rewriting.
- Refine current canonical documents only where evidence shows an interpretation gap.
- Introduce responsibility names through relational explanations, not mass renaming.
- Require every canonical change to identify which existing responsibility it clarifies.
- Preserve exact source hierarchy, authority, checkpoint, evidence, and transition state.
- Do not let one descriptive level replace another.
- Treat correction as localized refinement unless evidence proves a governing-spine change.

## Architectural continuity conclusion

The repository preserves one coherent governing spine.

The project is not a collection of unrelated systems joined after the fact.

Its current and historical artifacts consistently preserve different views of the same governed movement:

```text
reality
-> understanding and specification
-> requirements and evidence
-> authority and authorization
-> workflow and execution crossing
-> bounded completion
-> return and reconnection
```

Governance protects the legitimacy of the complete relationship.

The remaining continuity work is to make that relationship easier to read across canonical present-state documentation without erasing the development history that proves how it was reached.
