## Source-bounded lexical note

The Sanskrit terms below carry different shades of meaning across textual and philosophical traditions. The mappings here are intentionally narrow and lexical, not claims that one tradition historically anticipated the NI AI architecture.

- **Vāc (वाच् / vāc)** — speech, voice, word, statement, expression.
- **Vijñāna (विज्ञान / vijñāna)** — distinguishing or discerning, understanding, recognizing, intelligence, knowledge; in some Buddhist contexts, consciousness or thought-faculty.
- **Jñāna (ज्ञान / jñāna)** — knowledge, cognizance, wisdom.
- **Prāṇa (प्राण / prāṇa)** — breath, vital spirit, vital air, life; this is the more direct lexical term for breath / vital-force / life-air language.

Therefore, **vijñāna should not be treated as a simple synonym for life force, breath, or air without a specific textual source**. Its safer correspondence in this thesis note is discernment / differentiated knowing / conscious cognition, depending on context.

## Conceptual correspondence

The useful architecture-level correspondence is not identity but separation of functions:

```text
JÑĀNA
knowledge / what is known

->

VIJÑĀNA
discernment / differentiated understanding / assessment

->

VĀC
speech / articulated expression / outward statement
```

A possible systems interpretation is:

```text
KNOWLEDGE
-> DISCERNMENT
-> EXPRESSION
```

The NI AI governance architecture adds a critical non-transfer rule:

```text
KNOWLEDGE != AUTHORITY
DISCERNMENT != PERMISSION
EXPRESSION != DIRECTIVE
PROPOSAL != EXECUTION AUTHORITY
```

The conceptual connection is therefore:

> **Meaning may pass forward while authority does not.**

A system may know something, discriminate among interpretations, and express a proposition without acquiring the legitimate right to authorize or execute a consequential action.
