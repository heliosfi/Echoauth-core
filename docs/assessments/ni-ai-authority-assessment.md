# NI AI Authority Assessment

## Status

Founder-authorized repository assessment.

Authority-assessment scope only.

This document does not authorize runtime, implementation, deployment, execution, trading, funding movement, autonomous action, command execution, or capability expansion.

## Governing question

How does the repository determine whether a specific consequential transition has legitimate authority to occur?

## Governing baseline

NI AI is built with governance.

Governance preserves the legitimacy of consequential transitions.

Authority is the explicit, bounded source that permits one named transition or act to be considered under its governing conditions.

Authority does not arise from intelligence, readiness, evidence, chronology, capability, repository presence, workflow position, or a plausible next step.

## Authority source

The repository establishes founder authority as the governing authority source for consequential repository movement.

A repository, document, system, executor, agent, gate result, capability, stage, or workflow position cannot create its own authority.

This is the first boundary in the authority lane:

```text
Need, readiness, evidence, or capability
!= authority
```

Assessment: explicit and aligned.

## Authority must be exact

Valid authority must identify the exact governed subject and, as applicable:

- founder and authority identity;
- repository and branch;
- locked checkpoint and ancestry rule;
- exact purpose and governing question;
- exact allowed outputs, paths, and actions;
- exact prohibited actions;
- executor and task/session identity;
- delegation and independence rules;
- prerequisites and precedence;
- validation and completion conditions;
- expiry, suspension, revocation, supersession, consumption, and exhaustion;
- refusal and halt conditions;
- explicit non-authorization of adjacent movement.

Authority is therefore not a general expression of support.

It is a bounded engineering object tied to one legitimate transition or act.

Assessment: aligned with specificity before dispatch.

## Authority currentness

The repository treats authority as usable only while every required lifecycle condition remains valid.

Authority must fail closed when it is:

- not yet effective;
- stale;
- expired;
- consumed;
- exhausted;
- suspended;
- revoked;
- superseded;
- conflicting;
- over-scoped;
- improperly transferred;
- unverifiable.

Currentness is not inferred from recent publication or repository presence.

Assessment: aligned.

## Delegation

Delegation does not transfer final authority.

Any delegation or redelegation must be exact and explicit.

A delegate cannot:

- enlarge scope;
- renew authority;
- transfer it beyond the stated rule;
- redelegate without named permission;
- self-assign adjacent work;
- convert execution capability into permission.

Silence means delegation is prohibited where the controlling contract requires explicit delegation.

Assessment: aligned.

## Consumption and exhaustion

The repository treats authority as a lifecycle, not a permanent entitlement.

A single-use lane ends at its stated consumption event and becomes exhausted after its bounded completion or earlier terminating condition.

Consumption and exhaustion prevent:

- replay;
- continuation;
- adjacent work;
- authority inheritance;
- automatic follow-on movement.

A `PASS`, publication, completion, acceptance, closure, or clean repository state does not renew or broaden the consumed authority.

Assessment: aligned with non-automatic continuation.

## Stage authority lifecycle

The canonical stage lifecycle is:

```text
ENTRY
-> IMPORTANT ACTS
-> EXIT
```

Ordinary bounded activity may occur within an entered stage, but entry is not standing operational authority.

Each bounded lane still requires its own applicable authority.

Important Acts are acts that durably create, change, resolve, or record governing state, including authority issuance, narrowing, suspension, revocation, supersession, task-order issuance, evidence acceptance, stage entry, stage exit, conflict resolution, or closure.

Assessment: authority is connected to the durable effect of an act, not merely to the importance of its subject.

## Authority and non-action

The Authority Clarity Operating Law identifies the point where power is about to move while responsibility, authority, permission, refusal, mode, or risk remains unresolved.

Its core law is:

> Power must kneel to responsibility before it moves.

The repository recognizes refusal, hold, halt, `FAIL`, `BLOCKED`, and other protected non-action outcomes as legitimate completion states.

Authority therefore does not exist merely to enable action.

It also preserves when action must remain unavailable.

Assessment: aligned.

## Authority-mode chain

The repository's earlier authority-mode spine preserves a useful separation:

```text
Inputs and meaning
-> structural context
-> state and mode discipline
-> authorized state posture
-> EchoAuth permission enforcement
-> bounded execution
```

The current NI AI interpretation refines this without erasing the earlier evidence:

- Adumetric preserves and narrows meaning and requirements.
- Hawk carries workflow state.
- authority establishes whether the named movement has an explicit legitimate source;
- EchoAuth enforces permission under authority, policy, identity, and invariants;
- CEG controls the authorized crossing into one execution cycle;
- Saloherm performs the bounded downstream responsibility.

Authority is not identical to permission enforcement.

Authority is the explicit governing source that permission enforcement must validate and apply.

Assessment: boundary preserved.

## Relationship to governance

Governance and authority are related but distinct.

Governance preserves the system-wide laws that make transitions legitimate.

Authority supplies the explicit bounded permission source for one named consequential act or transition.

Governance determines what valid authority must satisfy.

Authority does not become governance simply because it controls an important act.

Assessment: aligned.

## Relationship to requirements

Requirements establish what must be true before a transition may be considered.

Satisfying requirements does not create authority.

Requirements may identify that founder authority, acceptance authority, entry authority, execution authority, or another exact authority is required.

The authority must still be separately issued, current, applicable, and bounded.

Assessment: aligned.

## Relationship to workflow

Hawk carries authority identity and status as workflow state.

Workflow may verify that required authority exists and remains usable before dispatch.

Workflow does not create, expand, renew, transfer, revoke, or supersede authority.

If authority becomes unavailable, workflow must refuse, hold, halt, or return the work according to the controlling contract.

Assessment: aligned.

## Relationship to EchoAuth

EchoAuth enforces permission boundaries.

It verifies whether the requested action, resource, identity, policy, payload, time, and runtime envelope are permitted under the applicable authority and invariants.

EchoAuth does not originate founder authority.

A valid authority record without successful permission enforcement does not create execution.

Successful permission enforcement cannot enlarge the authority it consumes.

Assessment: aligned.

## Relationship to CEG and Saloherm

CEG may control the crossing into execution only after the applicable authority and permission result are valid.

Saloherm may perform only the exact bounded responsibility carried by that authority and task order.

Neither CEG nor Saloherm may infer adjacent authority from capability, completion, or discovered need.

Assessment: aligned.

## Primary findings

### Finding 1 — The authority architecture already exists

The repository contains a mature authority lifecycle distributed across founder orders, authority records, task orders, stage doctrine, acceptance records, permission enforcement, and exhaustion rules.

Disposition: confirmed.

### Finding 2 — Authority is explicit and bounded, not ambient

There is no standing authority merely because a stage is entered, a repository exists, a capability is available, or prior work succeeded.

Disposition: aligned.

### Finding 3 — Authority is a consumable lifecycle object

Effectiveness, currentness, delegation, transfer, suspension, revocation, supersession, consumption, and exhaustion determine whether authority remains usable.

Disposition: aligned.

### Finding 4 — Authority and permission are distinct

Authority identifies the legitimate governing source and scope.

EchoAuth enforces whether a requested movement conforms to that authority and its invariants.

Disposition: aligned.

### Finding 5 — Authority protects non-action as well as action

When authority is unclear, stale, absent, conflicting, or unverifiable, refusal or halt is the correct governed result.

Disposition: aligned.

### Finding 6 — Completion never manufactures follow-on authority

Completion, validation, acceptance, indexing, synchronization, closure, or `PASS` resolves only its exact question and does not authorize the next lane.

Disposition: aligned.

## Authority assessment conclusion

The repository already demonstrates a coherent authority architecture.

The natural authority lane is:

```text
Consequential need
-> exact requirements
-> explicit bounded authority
-> lifecycle and currentness verification
-> workflow carriage
-> EchoAuth permission enforcement
-> CEG execution crossing
-> bounded completion
-> consumption and exhaustion
```

The repository does not need a newly invented authority subsystem.

It needs the existing authority discipline to remain connected to the NI AI spine and preserved as distinct from governance, requirements, workflow, permission enforcement, and execution.
