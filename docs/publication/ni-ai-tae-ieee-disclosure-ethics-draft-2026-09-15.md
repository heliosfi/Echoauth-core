## Candidate Ethics Considerations Response

This work is an architecture and adversarial-evaluation study based on governed repository source code, tests, contracts, and documentation. It examines risks arising when reasoning, proposals, state labels, prior permission, or returned evidence are treated as authority for a different stage without current, attributable validation. The evaluated risks include implicit authority transfer, unauthorized state substitution, stale permission, replay and repeated processing, execution-after-return, and overgeneralization from local tests.

The work seeks to reduce, not eliminate, specific authority-transfer risks. Three adversarial cases pass only at their tested seams, and two remain HOLD/PARTIAL because the legitimate end-to-end consumer chains required for complete evaluation are absent. The work does not establish universal AI safety, complete runtime validation, regulatory compliance, production readiness, or deployment authorization.

No human-subject experiment, participant recruitment, user intervention, private participant-data collection, medical or clinical experimentation, live production deployment, autonomous consequential execution, external-system action, or broker action is reported. This statement does not infer formal exemption, institutional-review-board status, or regulatory compliance.

The research may have dual-use implications. Descriptions of adversarial boundary failures can help evaluators identify weak governance seams, but may also inform attempts to exploit poorly governed systems. The manuscript limits operational exploitation detail and emphasizes defensive validation, bounded authority, fail-closed handling, evidence preservation, and explicit unresolved states.

Authority errors may affect people or institutions when automated recommendations or returned outputs are mistaken for current permission. Preserving human responsibility does not establish that every interface provides meaningful human control. No educational, demographic, user-impact, or field-study outcome was measured.

Repository evidence supplies traceability but does not by itself prove that every recorded claim is complete, correct, immutable, or authorized. Public release of precise repository paths may expose provenance, undermine anonymous review, or create misuse and security-review concerns. Any future anonymous evidence treatment must preserve the canonical record and must not delete, falsify, or silently conceal provenance. Sensitive credentials, private participant data, and operational secrets must not be included in a submission artifact.

The historical 194-test baseline remains historical and was not rerun or independently reproduced during manuscript reconstruction or formation of these candidate statements. No new runtime experiment was conducted, and no complete deployed runtime is established.
